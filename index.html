Keynotes, panels and conversations for each edition of the Circuit, from Port Louis to Nairobi.

Trademark Hotel, NairobiSingle track · panels only · up to 300 invited delegatesRequest a seat

Africa’s financial landscape is changing. Following Mauritius, The African Circuit moves to Nairobi to ask: how does Africa build the financial architecture to finance its own growth?

Two days. Eight conversations. From capital formation to the real economy.

Day 01 Tuesday 17 November · Capital

Who Will Finance Africa in the Next Global Cycle?

08:30–09:15

Registration and welcome coffee

09:15–09:25
Opening

Opening remarks

09:25–09:45
Keynote

Who Will Finance Africa’s Next Economic Chapter?

As global capital, trade and development finance evolve, what combination of domestic savings, development finance, private capital and international investment will finance Africa’s next phase of growth?

09:45–10:45
Panel 01

As Global Capital Reprices Risk, Can Africa Rely More on Its Own Savings?

Africa’s institutional capital pools are growing. What would allow more domestic savings to finance African businesses, infrastructure and productive assets while meeting the requirements of long-term institutional investors?

10:45–11:15

Networking coffee break

11:15–12:15
Panel 02

Who Sets the Price of African Risk?

How is African risk priced, and what would enable greater differentiation between markets, companies and issuers?

A conversation about ratings, information, local currency markets and the infrastructure required to deepen African capital markets.

12:15–13:15

Networking lunch

13:15–14:15
Panel 03

Can Africa Build Capital Markets That Depend Less on Banks and Foreign Capital?

Between traditional bank lending and public markets lies a significant financing gap.

Can private credit, corporate debt and structured capital create a deeper market for African companies, and what financial infrastructure is needed to support it?

14:15–14:45

Networking coffee break

14:45–15:45
Panel 04

If Development Finance Is Scarce, Where Should Every Dollar Take Risk?

Where can development finance have the greatest catalytic effect?

From direct investment and local currency financing to guarantees and risk sharing, this conversation examines how development capital can help mobilise larger pools of private investment.

Day 02 Wednesday 18 November · The Growth Economy

Can African Financial Infrastructure Match Africa’s Economic Ambitions?

08:30–09:15

Registration and welcome coffee

09:15–09:20
Opening

Welcome back

09:20–09:40
Keynote

Financial Access Has Expanded. Has Access to Capital?

Africa has transformed access to payments and financial services.

Can the next generation of financial infrastructure translate that progress into deeper credit markets, stronger businesses and companies capable of scaling across the continent?

09:40–10:40
Panel 05

Africa Built the Payment Rails. Who Builds the Credit Rails?

Africa’s payments revolution changed how money moves. What comes next?

This conversation examines the infrastructure required to move from payments at scale to credit at scale, from fintech and data to digital identity, underwriting and regulation.

10:40–11:10

Networking coffee break

11:10–12:10
Panel 06

Can Africa Finance Its SMEs Without Importing the Capital?

SMEs drive African economies, yet access to growth capital remains constrained.

Can domestic financial institutions, fintechs and private capital finance a greater share of African enterprise growth, and where can international and development capital have the greatest catalytic effect?

12:10–13:10

Networking lunch

13:10–14:10
Panel 07

Africa Bears the Climate Risk. Can It Capture the Investment Opportunity?

Africa’s climate financing needs are substantial. But need alone does not create an investable market.

What business models, financial structures and measurement systems could transform adaptation, resilience and natural capital into investable opportunities?

14:10–14:40

Networking coffee break

14:40–15:40
Panel 08

Africa Is Building a Continental Market. Who Will Build the Capital Corridors?

African companies increasingly operate across borders. Trade and supply chains are becoming more regional and continental.

Yet much of the financial architecture supporting them remains national.

What would allow capital to move alongside African trade?

Can Africa build a continental trading market without building a continental market for capital?

15:40
